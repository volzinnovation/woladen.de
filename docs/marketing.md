# woladen.de Marketing Strategy and Measurement

## Context

woladen.de is an EU-wide charging product covering the European Union plus Switzerland and Norway. The product is already feature-rich across web, iPhone, and Android:

- Web app with map search, route planning, filters, favorites, ratings, local notes, station details, and live status/pricing where available.
- Native iPhone app with MapKit, route planning, filters, local favorites/categories, iPad support, and Apple Maps handoff.
- Native Android app with Jetpack Compose, OSMDroid, route planning, filters, local favorites/categories, optional local bundle import, and external navigation handoff.
- Large SEO surface: about 439,830 generated sitemap URLs, including about 314,660 station pages, 119,002 city pages, 5,589 amenity pages, and localized pages across 23 languages.
- Open-static catalog scale: about 288,994 stations, 824,703 chargers, 179,785 fast chargers, and 159,430 fast chargers with amenities.

The marketing problem is therefore not lack of product surface. The problem is learning which channels and use cases produce real activation without compromising the privacy-first story.

## Core Positioning

Do not market woladen as another charging-station directory. The market already has maps.

The wedge is:

```text
Find EV charging stops where the people in the car can use the next 20-40 minutes well.
```

Use the canonical brand language:

```text
The human side of charging
Because charging time is your time
```

Practical product claims:

- Find chargers across Europe, not only Germany.
- Default to fast chargers from 50 kW.
- Filter by useful nearby places: toilets, cafes, bakeries, restaurants, supermarkets, playgrounds, parks, hotels, pharmacies, and more.
- Plan route-based charging stops.
- Save favorites locally on device.
- Open navigation in Apple Maps, Google Maps, or the platform default.
- Show live availability and pricing where providers publish usable data.
- No account, no ads, no cookies, no cross-site tracking.

Do not lead with:

- "EV map"
- "300k+ stations"
- "open data" as the main consumer promise
- "AI/MCP integration"

Those are supporting facts. The consumer promise is better charging stops.

## Market Priorities

The current product covers the European Union plus Switzerland and Norway. Focus effort where distribution can be earned cheaply, not merely where EV adoption is highest.

External market signals:

- ACEA reported battery-electric cars at 17.4% EU market share in 2025 and 19.4% in Q1 2026.
- IEA reported Europe reached just over 1 million public charging points in 2024, growing more than 35% year over year.
- EAFO's June 2026 news flash reported 1,155,861 publicly accessible recharging points in preliminary data covering 14 of 27 EU member states.
- WRI lists Norway, Sweden, Denmark, Finland, and the Netherlands among the fastest EV adopters globally.

Priority order:

| Priority | Market | Why | Primary approach |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | DACH: DE, AT, CH | German copy exists, DACH travel is cross-border, forums are active, and the product already has strong Germany/AT/CH coverage. | Founder-feedback posts, route pages, app-store custom pages, OSM QA. |
| 2 | France + Benelux | High charging density, strong cross-border travel, large route-planning pain around fragmented operators and travel corridors. | Route-specific landing pages, French/Dutch forum posts, localized store pages. |
| 3 | Nordics + Norway | Very high EV adoption and road-trip use, but incumbents are strong. | App-store positioning, route-specific content, selective forum/community feedback. |
| 4 | Iberia: ES, PT | Strong tourism/road-trip use case and good bundle coverage. | SEO route pages and Spanish/Portuguese app-store listings before forum-heavy outreach. |
| 5 | Central/Eastern EU corridors | Lower immediate EV density, but useful for long-tail SEO and cross-border travel. | Programmatic route/city/amenity pages, not manual community work first. |

### 2. OpenStreetMap community

OpenStreetMap should be treated as an open-data collaboration channel, not as a user-acquisition channel.

Good OSM angle:

```text
woladen is an open-source QA layer for EV charging stops and useful nearby amenities.
```

Bad OSM angle:

```text
Please use our app.
```

OSM actions:

- Add "Improve this place in OpenStreetMap" on station detail pages.
- Publish QA pages for missing or weak station/amenity data.
- Export reviewable CSV/GeoJSON for mappers.
- Create GitHub issues labeled `osm`, `data-quality`, `good-first-mapping-task`, and `needs-local-survey`.
- Start with discussion in the relevant OSM community before any organized task campaign.

Important boundaries:

- No automated imports.
- No mechanical edits.
- No adding woladen links to OSM objects as marketing.
- Do not import Bundesnetzagentur/NAP data into OSM without license clearance and community approval.
- Follow OSM Organised Editing Guidelines and Import Guidelines if work becomes coordinated.
- Use MapRoulette only after community discussion, and only for small review tasks that mappers can verify responsibly.

Useful OSM venues and references:

| Resource | URL | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| OSM Germany community forum | https://community.openstreetmap.org/c/communities/de/56 | First place for Germany-specific QA discussion. |
| OSM Community Forum | https://community.openstreetmap.org/ | Broader country/community discussions. |
| Organised Editing Guidelines | https://osmfoundation.org/wiki/Organised_Editing_Guidelines | Required if woladen coordinates mapping activity. |
| OSM Import Guidelines | https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Import/Guidelines | Required reading before any external data import. |
| MapRoulette docs | https://learn.maproulette.org/en-US/documentation/creating-a-challenge/ | Later option for mapper-reviewed microtasks. |

### 3. App stores

Use App Store and Google Play as trust and segmented acquisition surfaces.

The native apps are not thin wrappers; they have route planning, filters, favorites, categories, station details, and native map/navigation handoff. That is enough to promote them directly.

Apple supports up to 70 custom product pages. Google Play supports up to 50 custom store listings.

Create segmented pages/listings:

| Segment | iOS/Android listing angle | First screenshot |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Route planner | Better charging stops along your route. | Route planner results. |
| Families | Find chargers near toilets, food, and playgrounds. | Amenity-rich station detail. |
| DACH road trips | Better stops across Germany, Austria, Switzerland. | Route/city/country screenshot in German. |
| France/Benelux | Public fast chargers with useful nearby places. | French or Dutch localized station detail. |
| Nordics/Norway | Road-trip charging stops with useful places nearby. | Map or route planner. |
| Privacy-first | No account, no ads, no cookies. | Info/privacy/source screen. |

Do not spend meaningful paid budget until:

- search works reliably,
- route pages are shareable,
- app-store pages exist for the target segment,
- aggregate product-action measurement is in place.

If using paid credits, use exact-intent keywords only:

```text
ladesäulen app

```

Next SEO focus:

- route pages,
- corridor pages,
- amenity-on-route pages,
- high-quality pages for popular travel pairs,
- pages that are useful enough to share in forum replies.

### 5. GitHub and open-source distribution

Open source is useful for trust and contributors, but it is not enough as a consumer channel.

Use GitHub for:

- transparent issue labels for station corrections,
- data-quality tasks,
- OSM QA exports,
- public changelog after forum feedback,
- "good first issue" tasks around localization and route pages,
- credibility when posting in technical communities.

Do not lead consumer posts with GitHub. Lead with a concrete route or charging-stop problem.

## Recommendation

Use both Cloudflare and a tiny first-party aggregate metrics endpoint, but give them different jobs.

Do not use cross-site attribution, cookies, user IDs, fingerprinting, ad pixels, or session stitching.

### 1. Cloudflare for passive site intelligence

Use Cloudflare in front of GitHub Pages to get aggregate traffic, referrer, country, bot, uptime, cache, and performance signals that GitHub Pages does not expose.

This answers questions like:

- Which pages receive traffic?
- Which countries and referrers are visible at an aggregate level?
- Are bots or crawlers consuming most traffic?
- Are route/country/station landing pages reachable and fast?
- Are there spikes after forum posts, app-store updates, or launch announcements?

Recommended configuration:

- Proxy `woladen.de` through Cloudflare.
- Keep GitHub Pages as the origin.
- Use Cloudflare analytics/request data in aggregate.
- Avoid Cloudflare products that introduce tracking cookies unless there is a specific operational need.
- Update the privacy policy to disclose Cloudflare as an infrastructure and security provider.

Cloudflare is best for traffic and operations. It cannot tell whether users completed meaningful product actions such as route calculation, station detail opens, navigation clicks, or app-store clicks.

### 2. First-party aggregate endpoint for product activation

Add a minimal endpoint on a woladen-controlled backend, for example:

```text
POST https://live-eu.woladen.de/v1/metrics/event
```

The endpoint should increment daily aggregate counters and discard request-level data immediately.

Allowed event examples:

```text
page_view
search_submitted
search_no_results
route_calculated
station_opened
directions_clicked
favorite_added
app_store_clicked
google_play_clicked
feedback_clicked
```

Allowed low-risk dimensions:

```text
date
event
page_type
locale
country
station_country
platform
source
medium
campaign
result_count_bucket
route_distance_bucket
```

Avoid collecting:

```text
cookies
user IDs
session IDs
fingerprints
raw IP addresses
full user agents
precise coordinates
raw search queries
raw route origins or destinations
favorite names
ratings tied to a device/user identity
personal notes
```

For example, this is acceptable:

```json
{
  "event": "route_calculated",
  "platform": "web",
  "locale": "de",
  "source": "goingelectric",
  "campaign": "hamburg-copenhagen-feedback",
  "country": "DE-DK",
  "route_distance_bucket": "250-500km",
  "result_count_bucket": "10-25"
}
```

This is not acceptable:

```json
{
  "user_id": "abc123",
  "session_id": "xyz789",
  "origin": "Auguststr. 2, Berlin",
  "destination": "private home address",
  "lat": 52.523,
  "lon": 13.401,
  "user_agent": "full browser fingerprint"
}
```

This endpoint is not user attribution. It is aggregate product learning.

## Why Both Are Needed

Cloudflare can show that traffic arrived. It cannot show whether the product worked for the user.

The first-party endpoint can show that users performed meaningful actions. It does not need to identify users to do that.

Together they answer:

- Did a forum post create a traffic spike?
- Did that traffic calculate routes or only bounce?
- Which route pages convert to station detail opens?
- Which countries produce app-store clicks?
- Which campaigns produce no-result searches?
- Which product flows should be fixed before spending more effort on distribution?

## Privacy Promise

The public promise should stay simple:

```text
No cookies. No accounts. No ad tracking. No cross-site tracking.
```

More detailed version:

```text
woladen uses privacy-preserving, aggregate operational metrics to understand whether the app works: for example, counts of searches, route calculations, station opens, and navigation clicks. These metrics do not use cookies, user IDs, session IDs, fingerprinting, precise location logs, or personal route histories.
```

## Rollout Plan

1. Put Cloudflare in front of GitHub Pages.
2. Confirm the site, sitemap files, app badges, canonical URLs, and GitHub Pages TLS still work.
3. Update the privacy policy to mention Cloudflare as an infrastructure provider.
4. Add UTM-style campaign parameters to forum, app-store, and launch links, but do not persist them client-side.
5. Implement the first-party aggregate event endpoint.
6. Add web events first: route calculated, station opened, directions clicked, app-store clicked, search no-results.
7. Add iPhone and Android events only after the endpoint and privacy copy are stable.
8. Review weekly by aggregate totals, not user journeys.

## Decision

Do both.

Cloudflare gives passive traffic and operational visibility. The first-party aggregate endpoint gives product activation signals. Neither requires cookies, user accounts, ad tech, or user-level attribution.

The hard boundary is this: measure flows, not people.
